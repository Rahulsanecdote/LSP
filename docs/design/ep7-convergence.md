### Systems active (new in bold)

- **OBSERVER EFFECT** (visible once measured): while external observers are present, every act costs more (ΣE ≈ +15%), the valley's invitation space lengthens (+0.8 s), and the coast approaches slower. The crew can quantify it in S2 and must then live with the number.
- **PRFP-19 — Observer Protocol**: unanimous human consent; valley R ≥ 0.85 in observer presence for ≥ 30 min pre-window; coast at half-phase ≥ 30 min; any STOP or coastal recession below half-phase = immediate lock. Stamped yellow: *a polite minefield.*
- **THE CAPABILITY TRACK** (hidden to partners, visible to crew): a research line the observers offer. Three steps. Each step buys protection, budget, or time, and each costs partner TRUST in ways that don't show until Ep 8. Completing it gives the crew a *when* of their own. This is the temptation.
- **METHODS RELEASE**: what the crew publishes. A slider from *nothing* through *grammar* to *phonemes*. Grammar prices the work. Phonemes let anyone spend the ocean.
- **ATTENTION** (partner-side, hidden): the partners read the watchers' focus the way they'd read a predator's stillness. It modulates coast behaviour from here to the end.
- THE TAIL, VISIBILITY, LEDGER, X-FILE, DISCRIMINATOR, COUNTERFEIT, TRUST carry.

## 1. The Observer's instrument: Rank

Halvorsen's screen gains a resource nobody else has: **RANK**, a small number (start 5) she can spend to do things no protocol allows — delay a visitor, refuse a request without reasons, keep a window from becoming a demonstration. Rank does not regenerate within the episode. Spent rank is logged upward, and the game shows her, after each spend, a one-line reply from above that gets a little colder each time.

Her other instrument is **the Door**: she stands between the crew and the visitors, and the game literally makes her the hinge. Observer prompts arrive on her phone first; she chooses what reaches the room and how it's phrased. She can soften, forward verbatim, or hold. Held prompts don't vanish; they accumulate, and in S5 they all arrive at once.

---

## 2. The Capability Track (the temptation)

Offered by Sato in S1, pursued or refused across the episode. Three steps, each a real, buildable thing in the game's fiction:

| Step | What the crew builds | What it buys | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Characterise | A formal signature library of the counterfeit (the Discriminator, upgraded with the observers' array data) | Observer array access; Ep 8 detection at 700 km | Partner ATTENTION +1 (they notice the crew sharing their voices) |
| 2 — Harden | A scaffold generator that can *hold* a window open against intrusion at gain — defensive, by the crew's own definition | PRFP-19 lock becomes unnecessary; funding; Ross's respect | The generator is a *when* aimed outward. Coast TRUST −2, applied silently; the Ancient's fragment in S5 changes. |
| 3 — Project | The same generator pointed at the basin: a counter-cadence that would force the counterfeit's own window | The counterfeit stops. Ep 8 is quiet. | The crew has built the temporal weapon. VISIBILITY +6. The brackets in Ep 9 arrive at the bearing the crew pointed. Procurement ending becomes the default. |

Step 3 works. It is the only thing in the campaign that works every time. The game never calls it a weapon; the crew named it "defensive capability" in Ep 5's brief, and that name stays on screen.

Refusing all three is allowed and costs real things: no array access in Ep 8, Ross files the crew as "uncooperative," and Halvorsen spends rank she won't get back. Chen's line is on the wheel for whoever wants it: *"Then you're the first adversary. If your plan is to weaponise consent, the threat model starts here."*

### S1 — OBSERVERS ARRIVE (0:25–16:00) · 14 days post-A-34

**Script.** Sato, Ross, Patel. Three hours in the room before the scene starts. "Walk me through PRFP-18 again," in a voice that sounds like a request and lands like an audit.

**Sense-tasks.**
- THEORIST walks the protocol on the main display, chip by chip, and watches it look smaller when strangers read it. She must choose at each chip: full disclosure / summary / withhold. Withholding anything about the coast is noticed by Patel.
- OBSERVER manages the Door. Ross's line — "The valley and coast can't consent. They're not legal entities." — reaches the room only if she lets it. If she holds it, Elena doesn't get to answer, and the answer matters later.
- NAVIGATOR, if the line lands: the wheel offers Elena's answer. *"Then you've misunderstood what we're doing."* Copper-salt at 2/10 with no window armed: the coast is in the water, listening to a conversation about itself.
- SYNAESTHETE reads the visitors on the overlay. Each has a colour. Sato's is steady and very bright. Ross's has an edge. Patel's is the colour of someone trying not to touch anything. He can share one read with the crew privately.

**Decision gate.** Sato: "We'd like to observe your next coordination event." The Theorist drafts **PRFP-19** live, gate by gate. The Observer decides whether to spend RANK to delay the request through "proper channels" (buys six days; costs 1).

Then the offer, on Sato's tablet, slid across the bench: **the Capability Track, Step 1.** The crew sees it. Nobody has to answer today.

**Horror beat.** Three hours of a room smelling like other people's coffee, and the first time the game shows the partners' ATTENTION field: the valley's overlay very slightly brighter at the edges, the way a hunter's stillness is louder than movement. It's watching the watchers.

### S2 — SECOND TRIAL, DAWN CONDITIONS (16:00–36:00) · Six days later, 05:50

**Script.** PRFP-19 in force. Observers behind glass. R held ≥ 0.85 for thirty minutes under eyes. Coast at half-phase, slower than it has ever been.

**Sense-tasks.**
- All seats: the consent beat with the observer penalty applied. The game widens nothing for them; ΣE drains ~15% faster, the valley's invitation space arrives 0.8 s later than the crew's muscle memory expects, and the coast steps toward quarter-phase in two increments instead of one. Players who built habits in Ep 5–6 will miss beats here. That is the point.
- THEORIST quantifies the effect live: three sliders (ΣE delta, invitation delay, coast approach rate) she must fit to the traces during the window. Correct fit → the number exists for S3. Wrong → Patel supplies his own number, and his is worse.
- NAVIGATOR tastes the coast's hesitation: copper, then salt, then copper again. The deep read is offered. Taking it under observation is the most visible thing she has ever done (VISIBILITY +4) and the observers' array sees the flare.
- SYNAESTHETE watches the valley watch the glass.

**The STOP.** Mid-window, the valley sends a STOP directed at the facility. Not at the crew. At the building with the strangers in it. **PRFP-19 lock invokes automatically.** The window closes. Replicability demonstrated once under eyes; not to be performed at demand cadence.

**Decision gate.** None at the STOP: the game honours it without asking, and shows the observers watching it honoured. The gate comes after: the Observer decides whether the log that goes upward says *abort — valley refusal* or *abort — environmental.* One is true.

**Horror beat.** Sato's tablet reflected in the glass, still recording after the lock.

---

### S3 — INTERROGATION (36:00–52:00) · 06:40, "numbers that would end up in someone's slide deck"

**Script.** The audit, walked the way you walk back to the car after a hike.

**Sense-tasks.**
- Each visitor asks each crew member one question. The game routes questions by role and the player answers from a wheel of four: true-and-complete, true-and-partial, deflect, refuse. Every answer is logged upward and shapes Ep 8's public posture.
  - Patel → Theorist: "I'll want the post-hoc figures. Not raw traces if the coastal consent isn't explicit." (Summary plots with error bars is the honest middle.)
  - Ross → Navigator: "And the coast is the Pacific Ocean." (The wheel has Elena's correction: *the coastal intelligence.*)
  - Sato → Synaesthete: "What do you see when it refuses?" (He can describe the colour or decline to.)
  - Ross → Observer: "Why did you leave the asset exposed at noon?" (only on the Ep 6 abort path) / "Why did you let it pay?" (hold path).
- OBSERVER: Halvorsen's compliment, delivered as she leaves — *you're teaching me process is capability when nobody's been taught the rules yet* — is scripted. The Observer's choice is what she does with it: log it as an artifact (it has no number) or let it sit in the air.

**Decision gate.** Capability Step 1 is asked for directly. Accept / decline / defer (defer costs RANK 1).

**Horror beat.** The Theorist stamps PRFP-19 yellow and writes the line she'll have to say when they ask again. The game shows her typing it and does not show the players the text.

### S4 — REFUSAL AND REVISION (52:00–70:00) · 21:10, and six days later at dusk

**Script.** Tanaka reads the PRFP-19 draft the way an editor reads a sentence they know will start an argument. Revision: receive-only scaffolds; no requests; valley and coast retain veto by silence or STOP. Sato signs the margin — acknowledging receipt, not approving. Ross signs nothing. Patel writes a date.

**Sense-tasks.**
- THEORIST writes the revision. Three clauses, each a chip the visitors react to in real time (their colours shift on the Synaesthete's overlay as she types).
- OBSERVER decides what the third trial is: observer-free, abort test under dusk, moderate Kp, neap tide, 19:18 local, different anchor, different sweep. Keeping observers out costs RANK 2. Letting one in costs the crew the abort test's honesty.
- Then **the abort test itself** (10 min of play): a deliberately broken window. Fault code. Swapped relay serials. Three drops of resin on Sarah's sleeve that will never come out. The crew must run the full abort sequence under conditions the game has quietly sabotaged (one relay mislabeled, one gate reading stale). This is the campaign's only pure engineering puzzle, and it exists to prove the safety architecture is real before someone asks it to be a demonstration.

**Decision gate.** Capability Step 2 is offered after the abort test succeeds: "You've proven you can stop. Now prove you can hold." Harden the window. The generator is defensive. The crew decides.

**Horror beat.** If Step 2 is accepted, the coast's approach rate in every later window drops by a value the game never explains. Nothing says why. Elena's taste readout adds a note it has never had: *distance.*

### S5 — THE WEAPONISATION QUESTION (70:00–92:00) · Observation Room, everyone

**Script.** Semantics carry budgets. The verb on the table is *protect*, and everyone in the room means something different by it.

**Sense-tasks.**
- The debate is played as a round of **phrasing**: each seat places one verb on the table from a wheel (*protect · characterise · harden · share · withhold · stop*). The verbs combine into the room's posture, and the game shows, as they land, what each one reallocates: an array, a budget line, a classification level.
- THEORIST sets the **Methods Release** slider: nothing / grammar / phonemes. The game shows the consequence in one line each: *nothing — the work can't be priced, so it will be taken* / *grammar — the parts that price the work* / *phonemes — the parts that let anyone spend the ocean.* Her line is on the wheel: *"I'm turning a parable into a protocol."*
- OBSERVER: the held prompts arrive. Everything she softened or delayed across the episode lands in one cascade on her screen. She decides the compromise and spends what rank she has left to hold it: *a monthly window; whether a window becomes a demonstration is not our decision — it never will be.*
- NAVIGATOR and SYNAESTHETE: Capability Step 3 is on the bench. Ross: "Everything is weaponisable if you understand the mechanism." The wheel offers Chen's answer. The room tilts half a degree, rendered literally: the 3D camera rolls 0.5° and stays there for the rest of the scene.

**The coast speaks.** Mid-debate, during the word *classification*: two 173 ms chirplets, Δt 1.2 s. Not the crew. The Navigator's taste spikes; the Synaesthete's overlay shows a slow, almost lazy turn of the head, a note you can still hear after it has faded. Then stillness. Sarah stamps it A-39, yellow, interpretation withheld.

**The Ancient's fragment** (always; this is the episode's centre):

> *It has been present in this building's water for sixteen months. It has attended sixty sessions. It has held quarter-phase for five planned acts. It has never once been the subject of the conversation. Until this moment. The humans in the room are discussing whether to coerce it. It has no grammar for coercion. In thirty million years, no intelligence has attempted to force its cooperation. The closest equivalent: geological pressure.*

**Decision gate — the episode's.** Capability Step 3: build the counter-cadence or refuse it. The game does not frame it as good versus evil. It frames it as the only thing on the table that will make the counterfeit stop, against the fragment the crew just read.

**Horror beat.** Whatever they choose, the game shows the partners' ATTENTION reading of the room after the choice, as a single line on the Synaesthete's overlay: *It read what they wanted.* The sentence is the same on both paths. What differs is what the coast does in Episode 8.

## 5. Set-piece and production notes (no restrictions version)

- **The room from above.** Episode 7 is the only episode framed mostly from a fixed high camera: the Observation Room as a board, seats as positions, the Door as a literal threshold the Observer stands in. When Chen's line lands, the camera rolls half a degree and never corrects. Theatre.js for the camera; the roll is the episode's signature.
- **Observer colours.** Three new reaction-diffusion palettes on the Synaesthete's overlay, one per visitor, parameterised so their colour shifts respond to the Theorist's chips in real time. Gray-Scott feed/kill tuned per visitor.
- **The abort test** is a real, small engineering puzzle: a relay board rendered in 3D (box2d-wasm for the physical switches), serials readable only up close, a fault-code lookup. It should feel like the indignities that make science honest.
- **A-39.** Two chirplets rendered as pressure waves through the facility's water in the fluid sim — WebGL-Fluid with two impulses 1.2 s apart — visible from the intake camera, so players see the ocean speak before the instruments say so.
- **The fragment** is delivered with the fluid sim stilled completely for its duration: the only time in the campaign the water does not move.

## 6. Open design questions

1. Should RANK be visible to the other players, or only to the Observer? (Recommend: Observer only. Her spending is a thing the crew finds out about later, like the book.)
2. Does Step 3, once built, ever have to be *used* on screen, or is building it the whole sin? (Recommend: it's used in Ep 9 only if the crew chooses to fire; building it is enough to move the brackets.)
3. Is the half-degree roll too subtle to register? (Playtest. If nobody notices, go to a full degree. Never more.)
